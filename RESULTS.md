# MOSEK search for the KKT/flag certificate

## Outcome

No exact SOS certificate for equation (12) was found.

The search did find and validate the correct symmetry-reduced SDP hierarchy,
including the KKT Hessian blocks that were only schematic in (12).  MOSEK
produces steadily improving negative lower bounds through degree 18, but the
bound is still strictly below zero.  Higher-degree runs become numerically
singular at the ONB equality face, and no rational PSD solution was obtained.
Accordingly, none of the numerical output is claimed as a proof of
copositivity.

## Normalization

In the isotropic branch,

```text
T = 1 - 9 p4 + 6 p6 = (3/16) E,
```

where `p4 = E[(X.Y)^4]` and `p6 = E[(X.Y)^6]`.  The SDP minimizes
the formal expectation of `T`.  A value below zero is a relaxation gap, not a
counterexample measure.

## Certificate cone encoded in `sos_search.py`

- exact antipodal/isotropic contraction of multigraph moments;
- one-root `O(2)` flag-square blocks in every tangent harmonic sector;
- two-root even and orientation-odd flag-square blocks;
- root-pair localizing blocks;
- ordinary harmonic positivity `A_l >= 0`;
- weight KKT identities `U(X)=E`;
- geometric-gradient KKT identities;
- both tangent directions of the spherical Hessian;
- four-sample, matrix-valued Hessian multipliers;
- global tangent trial-point gaps;
- the four-vector rank identities generated by
  `det Gram(X1,X2,X3,X4)=0` and invariant monomial multiples.

The rank identities are essential.  Without them the degree-14 relaxation has
a conspicuous pseudo-solution near

```text
p4 = 2/7,  p6 = 20/77,  T = -1/77.
```

That pseudo-solution is not evidence of an actual measure.

## Reproducible MOSEK results

Using the full valid hierarchy without the diagnostic box cuts:

| total degree | formal labels | MOSEK lower bound for `T` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 172 | -0.0019132779818384904 |
| 14 | 399 | -0.0010936259535694326 |
| 16 | 635 | -0.0006075411462358460 |
| 18 | 1224 | -0.0001659561850924085 |

Example:

```sh
python3 sos_search.py \
  --dual --summary-only --rank-relations \
  --degree 18 --no-pointwise-sos \
  --harmonics --three-point-flags --four-point-flags --two-root-flags \
  --gradient --potential --potential-matrices \
  --hessian --four-point-hessian \
  --global-gap --global-tangent-gaps \
  --tolerance 1e-8
```

The ONB equality functional can be audited against every block and identity:

```sh
python3 sos_search.py \
  --check-onb --rank-relations \
  --degree 14 --no-pointwise-sos \
  --harmonics --three-point-flags --four-point-flags --two-root-flags \
  --gradient --potential --potential-matrices \
  --hessian --four-point-hessian \
  --global-gap --global-tangent-gaps
```

This gives target value zero, KKT/rank residuals below `3e-15`, and PSD
violations no larger than floating-point roundoff.

## Important rejected numerical lead

Adding the valid inequalities `|M| <= 1` for every formal monomial makes the
degree-14 dual value appear nonnegative.  It does **not** yield an exact
zero-energy certificate: every nonconstant ONB monomial has `|M| < 1`, so
complementarity forces all such multipliers to vanish in any certificate sharp
at the ONB.  The corresponding primal solve is ill-conditioned and cannot be
rationally repaired.  Those diagnostic cuts are therefore excluded from the
table above.

## What remains

The computational evidence suggests convergence toward zero, but it does not
replace the missing analytic step.  A proof still needs one of:

1. a finite degree at which the exact primal cone contains `T`;
2. a closed-form family of certificates whose error tends to zero;
3. an independent rigidity/classification argument for the residual KKT
   pseudo-moment branch.

## Higher-arity extension

The hierarchy now also contains systematic higher-arity blocks:

- five- and six-point conditional star-flag squares;
- root-weighted five-/six-point flags, which also contain bounded polynomial
  factors among the shared roots;
- five-/six-point matrix-valued KKT Hessian multipliers;
- matrix forms of the four-vector Gram-rank identities on the higher-point
  flag spaces;
- exact ONB facial reduction and optional constraint scaling.

The ordinary star blocks give a clear nested improvement at degree 16 before
the solver reaches its singular accuracy floor:

| degree 16 flag layer | reported dual objective |
|---|---:|
| selected four-point hierarchy | -0.0006075411462358460 |
| five-point star flags | -0.0004403048051331293 |
| six-point star flags | -0.0003577419200269105 |

The strongest numerical result came from degree 14 with five-point
root-weighted flags and root-factor degree 2.  After rescaling mathematically
equivalent constraints, MOSEK reported

```text
T >= -4.778814766126516e-8
```

with maximum free-equality residual `2.66e-9`, maximum scalar-relation
residual `1.93e-10`, and minimum moment-block eigenvalue `-9.89e-10`.

This is a near-certificate, not an SOS proof.  The exact and perturbed primal
reconstruction attempts failed on the singular ONB face, including after
explicitly parametrizing every PSD matrix on its ONB nullspace.  No rational
PSD matrices were recovered and no certificate archive is provided.

## Exactification audit of the degree-14 five-point run

The exactification path now performs three operations before calling MOSEK:

1. reconstruct every ONB moment matrix and its kernel over \(\mathbb Q\);
2. eliminate the unrestricted KKT and Gram-rank directions by rational RREF;
3. intersect with the exact pole–equator equality functional.

The pole–equator moments are evaluated as Fourier constant terms.  The
corresponding rooted potential has the closed form

```text
U(z) = 4 q (1-q)^2,  q = (z.e)^2,
```

so this is a valid KKT equality face independently of the conjecture.

For the stated degree/arity/root-factor parameters, the audit finds:

```text
formal labels                 574
free equality generators       74
exact equality rank            71
quotient equations            503
```

After the ONB and pole–equator faces, the reduced primal still does not return
a finite feasible point. At tolerance `1e-9`, MOSEK's trace-minimization
iterates reached objective about `1.36e3` while the feasibility residual
decreased toward `1e-9`; the solver terminated with status `UNKNOWN`.
Without the pole–equator face the same trajectory occurs. An auxiliary
zero-target dual face exposes further very small eigen-directions, but cutting
them numerically makes the quotient inconsistent and therefore cannot be used
as proof data.

This is evidence of a non-attained/closure certificate, not an exact
degree-14 certificate. It is not, by itself, a theorem that no alternative
degree-14 representation exists; it does prove that the published
floating-point dual vector is insufficient data for rationalization.

The exact audit is reproduced by:

```sh
python3 sos_search.py \
  --check-onb --facial-reduce-onb --exact-onb-face \
  --pole-equator-faces continuous --eliminate-free \
  --degree 14 --no-pointwise-sos \
  --harmonics --three-point-flags --four-point-flags --two-root-flags \
  --max-flag-arity 5 --max-root-factor-degree 2 \
  --gradient --potential --potential-matrices \
  --hessian --four-point-hessian \
  --global-gap --global-tangent-gaps --rank-relations
```

The higher-arity search is reproduced by:

python3 sos_search.py \
