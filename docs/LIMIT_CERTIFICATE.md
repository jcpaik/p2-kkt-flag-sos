# The infinite-degree primal: which functions must be found

This document writes out the $\deg\to\infty$ limit of the primal certificate.
At finite degree the unknowns are Gram matrices; in the limit they become
*functions*, and the infinite-dimensional family of zero-energy measures
established in [Exact structure](STRUCTURE.md) turns complementary slackness
into an explicit system of functional equations on them.

Throughout, $\mu$ is an antipodally symmetric probability measure on $S^2$,
$U_\mu(z)=\int K(z\cdot y)\,d\mu(y)$, $q_\mu(z)=U_\mu(z)-E(\mu)$, and
$\mathcal Z_0$ denotes the set of zero-energy measures, i.e. the $SO(3)$-orbit of

$$\mu^*=\tfrac13\delta_{\pm e}+\tfrac23\nu,
\qquad \nu \text{ on } C=e^{\perp},\quad \hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0 .$$

## 1. The identity to be produced

All terms are homogenised with $\int d\mu=1$ so that both sides are polynomial
functionals of $\mu$ of matched degree. The certificate is an identity holding
for every admissible $\mu$:

$$E(\mu)
=\underbrace{\sum_{r,\alpha}\int_{(S^2)^r}
   \Big(\int \Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;y)\,d\mu(y)\Big)^{2}d\mu^{\otimes r}(R)}_{\text{flag squares}}
+\underbrace{\int_{(S^2)^m} P\,d\mu^{\otimes m}}_{P\,\ge\,0\ \text{pointwise}}
+\ \mathcal K(\mu)\ +\ \mathcal Z(\mu),$$

$$\mathcal K(\mu)=
\int \operatorname{tr}\!\big(B_\mu(x)\operatorname{Hess}_{S^2}U_\mu(x)\big)d\mu(x)
+\int_{S^2}\rho_\mu(z)\,q_\mu(z)\,d\sigma(z)
+\int a_\mu(x)q_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x)
+\int v_\mu(x)\cdot\nabla_{S^2}U_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x),$$

with $\mathcal Z(\mu)\equiv0$ identically.

## 2. The unknown functions

| object | domain | constraint |
|---|---|---|
| $\Phi_{r,\alpha}(x_1,\dots,x_r;y)$ | $(S^2)^r\times S^2$ | symmetric in the roots, correct antipodal parity, $r\ge0$ |
| $P(x_1,\dots,x_m)$ | $(S^2)^m$ | symmetric, **$P\ge0$ pointwise** |
| $B_\mu(x)$ | tangent plane at $x$ | $B_\mu(x)\succeq0$ |
| $\rho_\mu(z)$ | $S^2$ | $\rho_\mu\ge0$ |
| $a_\mu(x)$, $v_\mu(x)$ | $S^2$, tangent field | **free sign** (multiply $q_\mu=0$, $\nabla U_\mu=0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$) |
| $\Lambda_\mu$ | traceless symmetric $3\times3$ | free (multiplies $\int xx^{\mathsf T}d\mu-\tfrac13I=0$) |

Only $\Phi$, $P$, $B$, $\rho$ carry sign constraints; the rest are unrestricted
because they multiply identities.

$\mathcal Z$ is generated by $|x_i|^2=1$, antipodal parity, exchangeability, and
$\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_i,x_j,x_k,x_l)=0$ — the last is the only place where
$\dim=3$ enters the algebra.

## 3. Structural constraint from the geometry

$P\ge0$ is required on the semialgebraic set of realisable Gram matrices, so by
Positivstellensatz $P$ lies in the quadratic module generated by the principal
minors $\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_i:i\in S)$, $|S|\le3$, with sum-of-squares
coefficients.

## 4. The equality equations

Complementary slackness against every $\mu^*\in\mathcal Z_0$ gives the following.

### (E1) Every flag square vanishes on the support

$$\int\Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;y)\,d\mu^*(y)=0
\qquad\text{for every } R\in(\operatorname{supp}\mu^*)^r .$$

Write $g=\Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;\cdot)$ and let $\varphi$ be the angle along $C$.
Because $\nu$ ranges over *all* measures on $C$ with $\hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0$,
the number $\int g\,d\nu$ must be constant on that convex set. Equivalently:

> **(i)** $g|_C$ has only the Fourier modes $0,\pm2,\pm6$ — mode $4$ and all
> modes $\ge8$ must vanish;
>
> **(ii)** $\hat g_0(C)=-\tfrac12\,g(e)$.

This is the master functional equation. Two consequences:

* **Arity two is useless.** For $r=0$, (i) must hold for *every* great circle.
  Restricting a degree-$\ell$ harmonic to a circle produces mode $\ell$, and the
  "$m=\pm4$" coefficient functionals span $H_6^{*}$ under rotation, so (i) forces
  $\Phi_0\in H_0\oplus H_2$; then (ii) kills the constant. So $\Phi_0$ is a pure
  degree-2 harmonic, whose $\mu$-average vanishes identically under isotropy.
  The `empty_type_flag` block can contribute nothing.
* **The constraint count collapses with arity.** For $r=1$ with root $x$,
  (i)–(ii) must hold on the whole one-parameter family of circles through $x$,
  plus $x^{\perp}$. For $r=2$ with a generic non-orthogonal pair of roots there
  is exactly **one** admissible circle. This is the likeliest explanation for
  the five orders of magnitude gained by the five-point weighted flags over the
  three- and four-point layers.

### (E2) The pointwise part vanishes on the support

$P(x_1,\dots,x_m)=0$ whenever all $x_i\in\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$. For $m=3$,
with $u=x\cdot z$, $v=y\cdot z$, $t=x\cdot y$ and

$$D(u,v,t)=1+2uvt-u^2-v^2-t^2=\det\operatorname{Gram}(x,y,z),$$

three equatorial points are coplanar, so $P_3$ vanishes on the irreducible
surface $\{D=0\}$ and therefore is divisible by $D$:

$$\boxed{\;P_3=D\cdot R,\qquad R\ge0 \text{ on the domain},\qquad
R\big|_{\{u=v=0\}}=R\big|_{\{v=t=0\}}=R\big|_{\{t=u=0\}}=0\;}$$

The last conditions come from the triples (pole, equator, equator), where
$D=1-t^2\neq0$; since $R\ge0$, it vanishes to order $\ge2$ on those three lines.

### (E3) The global-gap multiplier

$\rho_{\mu^*}$ is supported on $\{U_{\mu^*}=E\}$. For the ONB,
$U(z)=32z_1^2z_2^2z_3^2$, so that zero set is the union of the three great
circles $\{z_i=0\}$ — one-dimensional, while $\operatorname{supp}\mu_{\rm ONB}$
is only three points. For pole + Haar equator, $U\propto q(1-q)^2$ with
$q=(z\cdot e)^2$, so the zero set is the equator together with the poles.

### (E4) The Hessian multiplier

$B_{\mu^*}(x)\operatorname{Hess}U_{\mu^*}(x)=0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$.
Note that at the ONB the potential vanishes to order **four** along every
tangent direction, $U\sim 32\,v_1^2v_2^2s^4$, so $\operatorname{Hess}U\equiv0$
on the support: the second-order KKT condition is *vacuous* there and places no
constraint on $B$. At equator points of the pole + Haar measure the Hessian has
one zero eigenvalue (along the equator) and one positive one, so $B$ is pinned
to the equatorial direction.

## 5. What assuming $\varepsilon=0$ additionally requires

The finite-degree data measured for the degree-14 five-point relaxation is

| $\varepsilon$ | minimal Gram trace |
|---:|---:|
| $3\times10^{-1}$ | 4.543 |
| $1\times10^{-1}$ | 6.016 |
| $5\times10^{-2}$ | 7.269 |
| $3\times10^{-2}$ | no converged solution |

so assuming that the $\varepsilon=0$ solution exists is assuming

$$\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\ \sum_{r,\alpha}\big\|\Phi^{(\varepsilon)}_{r,\alpha}\big\|_{L^2(\mu^{\otimes(r+1)})}^{2}<\infty .$$

If that supremum is infinite the correct object is not a certificate but the
asymptotic family of [Certificate theorem](CERTIFICATE_THEOREM.md) §7 — the
certificate then exists only as a limit, which is what the degree-14 evidence
currently indicates.

## 6. Summary

Find:

1. a countable family of leaf functions $\Phi_{r,\alpha}$ obeying the
   great-circle Fourier conditions (i) and (ii) on every circle compatible with
   their roots;
2. a pointwise nonnegative $P$, divisible by the three-point Gram determinant,
   whose quotient vanishes to order two on the three orthogonality lines;
3. a positive semidefinite tangent field $B$ annihilating $\operatorname{Hess}U_\mu$
   on the support;
4. a nonnegative $\rho$ supported on $\{U_\mu=E\}$;
5. free multipliers $a_\mu$, $v_\mu$, $\Lambda_\mu$;

such that the expansion of all of them in the multigraph moment algebra, modulo
$\det\operatorname{Gram}_4=0$, reproduces the target coefficient vector
$\big(\tfrac{16}{3},\,-48,\,32\big)$ on $\big(\text{constant},\,\mathrm{pair}_4,\,\mathrm{pair}_6\big)$.
