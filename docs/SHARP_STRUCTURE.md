# Sharp-certificate structure theory

This document is the structure theory of the sharp certificate: what the
$\deg\to\infty$ limit of the primal certificate must look like, the
complementary-slackness equations it must satisfy, the closed-form
solution of those equations at arities one and two (unweighted and
weighted), and the collective-transport second variation.  Background,
definitions (labels, flags, blocks, degree/arity), the certificate
framework, and the zero-energy family are in
[FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md); the enrichment machinery this theory
feeds (weights, cuts, theta atoms, Jensen/fiber-Toeplitz) is in
[ENRICHMENTS.md](ENRICHMENTS.md); the measurement record and the
cylindrical theorems are in
[SUBCASES_AND_RECORD.md](SUBCASES_AND_RECORD.md).

Throughout, $\mu$ is an antipodally symmetric probability measure on
$S^2$, $K(t)=32t^6-48t^4+20t^2-\tfrac43$,
$U_\mu(z)=\int K(z\cdot y)\,d\mu(y)$, $q_\mu(z)=U_\mu(z)-E(\mu)$, and
$\mathcal Z_0$ denotes the set of zero-energy measures, i.e. the
$SO(3)$-orbit of

$$\mu^*=\tfrac13\delta_{\pm e}+\tfrac23\nu,
\qquad \nu \text{ on } C=e^{\perp},\quad \hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0 .$$

# Part I. Non-attainment and the limit certificate

At finite degree the unknowns of the primal certificate are Gram
matrices; in the limit they become *functions*, and the
infinite-dimensional family $\mathcal Z_0$ turns complementary
slackness into an explicit system of functional equations on them.

## I.1 The identity to be produced

All terms are homogenised with $\int d\mu=1$ so that both sides are
polynomial functionals of $\mu$ of matched degree.  The certificate is
an identity holding for every admissible $\mu$:

$$E(\mu)
=\underbrace{\sum_{r,\alpha}\int_{(S^2)^r}
   \Big(\int \Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;y)\,d\mu(y)\Big)^{2}d\mu^{\otimes r}(R)}_{\text{flag squares}}
+\underbrace{\int_{(S^2)^m} P\,d\mu^{\otimes m}}_{P\,\ge\,0\ \text{pointwise}}
+\ \mathcal K(\mu)\ +\ \mathcal Z(\mu),$$

$$\mathcal K(\mu)=
\int \operatorname{tr}\!\big(B_\mu(x)\operatorname{Hess}_{S^2}U_\mu(x)\big)d\mu(x)
+\int_{S^2}\rho_\mu(z)\,q_\mu(z)\,d\sigma(z)
+\int a_\mu(x)q_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x)
+\int v_\mu(x)\cdot\nabla_{S^2}U_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x),$$

with $\mathcal Z(\mu)\equiv0$ identically.

## I.2 The unknown functions

| object | domain | constraint |
|---|---|---|
| $\Phi_{r,\alpha}(x_1,\dots,x_r;y)$ | $(S^2)^r\times S^2$ | symmetric in the roots, correct antipodal parity, $r\ge0$ |
| $P(x_1,\dots,x_m)$ | $(S^2)^m$ | symmetric, **$P\ge0$ pointwise** |
| $B_\mu(x)$ | tangent plane at $x$ | $B_\mu(x)\succeq0$ |
| $\rho_\mu(z)$ | $S^2$ | $\rho_\mu\ge0$ |
| $a_\mu(x)$, $v_\mu(x)$ | $S^2$, tangent field | **free sign** (multiply $q_\mu=0$, $\nabla U_\mu=0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$) |
| $\Lambda_\mu$ | traceless symmetric $3\times3$ | free (multiplies $\int xx^{\mathsf T}d\mu-\tfrac13I=0$) |

Only $\Phi$, $P$, $B$, $\rho$ carry sign constraints; the rest are
unrestricted because they multiply identities.

$\mathcal Z$ is generated by $|x_i|^2=1$, antipodal parity,
exchangeability, and $\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_i,x_j,x_k,x_l)=0$ —
the last is the only place where $\dim=3$ enters the algebra.

## I.3 Structural constraint from the geometry

$P\ge0$ is required on the semialgebraic set of realisable Gram
matrices, so by Positivstellensatz $P$ lies in the quadratic module
generated by the principal minors
$\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_i:i\in S)$, $|S|\le3$, with sum-of-squares
coefficients.

## I.4 The equality equations

Complementary slackness against every $\mu^*\in\mathcal Z_0$ gives the
following.

### (E1) Every flag square vanishes on the support

$$\int\Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;y)\,d\mu^*(y)=0
\qquad\text{for every } R\in(\operatorname{supp}\mu^*)^r .$$

Write $g=\Phi_{r,\alpha}(R;\cdot)$ and let $\varphi$ be the angle along
$C$.  Because $\nu$ ranges over *all* measures on $C$ with
$\hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0$, the number $\int g\,d\nu$ must be constant
on that convex set.  Equivalently:

> **(i)** $g|_C$ has only the Fourier modes $0,\pm2,\pm6$ — mode $4$
> and all modes $\ge8$ must vanish;
>
> **(ii)** $\hat g_0(C)=-\tfrac12\,g(e)$.

This is the master functional equation.  Two consequences:

* **Arity two is useless.**  For $r=0$, (i) must hold for *every*
  great circle.  Restricting a degree-$\ell$ harmonic to a circle
  produces mode $\ell$, and the "$m=\pm4$" coefficient functionals span
  $H_6^{*}$ under rotation, so (i) forces $\Phi_0\in H_0\oplus H_2$;
  then (ii) kills the constant.  So $\Phi_0$ is a pure degree-2
  harmonic, whose $\mu$-average vanishes identically under isotropy.
  The `empty_type_flag` block can contribute nothing.
* **The constraint count collapses with arity.**  For $r=1$ with root
  $x$, (i)–(ii) must hold on the whole one-parameter family of circles
  through $x$, plus $x^{\perp}$.  For $r=2$ with a generic
  non-orthogonal pair of roots there is exactly **one** admissible
  circle.  This is the likeliest explanation for the five orders of
  magnitude gained by the five-point weighted flags over the three- and
  four-point layers.

### (E2) The pointwise part vanishes on the support

$P(x_1,\dots,x_m)=0$ whenever all $x_i\in\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$.
For $m=3$, with $u=x\cdot z$, $v=y\cdot z$, $t=x\cdot y$ and

$$D(u,v,t)=1+2uvt-u^2-v^2-t^2=\det\operatorname{Gram}(x,y,z),$$

three equatorial points are coplanar, so $P_3$ vanishes on the
irreducible surface $\{D=0\}$ and therefore is divisible by $D$:

$$\boxed{\;P_3=D\cdot R,\qquad R\ge0 \text{ on the domain},\qquad
R\big|_{\{u=v=0\}}=R\big|_{\{v=t=0\}}=R\big|_{\{t=u=0\}}=0\;}$$

The last conditions come from the triples (pole, equator, equator),
where $D=1-t^2\neq0$; since $R\ge0$, it vanishes to order $\ge2$ on
those three lines.

### (E3) The global-gap multiplier

$\rho_{\mu^*}$ is supported on $\{U_{\mu^*}=E\}$.  For the ONB,
$U(z)=32z_1^2z_2^2z_3^2$, so that zero set is the union of the three
great circles $\{z_i=0\}$ — one-dimensional, while
$\operatorname{supp}\mu_{\rm ONB}$ is only three points.  For pole +
Haar equator, $U\propto q(1-q)^2$ with $q=(z\cdot e)^2$, so the zero
set is the equator together with the poles.

### (E4) The Hessian multiplier

$B_{\mu^*}(x)\operatorname{Hess}U_{\mu^*}(x)=0$ on
$\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$.  Note that at the ONB the potential
vanishes to order **four** along every tangent direction,
$U\sim 32\,v_1^2v_2^2s^4$, so $\operatorname{Hess}U\equiv0$ on the
support: the second-order KKT condition is *vacuous* there and places
no constraint on $B$.  At equator points of the pole + Haar measure the
Hessian has one zero eigenvalue (along the equator) and one positive
one, so $B$ is pinned to the equatorial direction.

## I.5 Non-attainment: the $\varepsilon$-trace pole

Requiring the certificate to close at $\varepsilon=0$ is requiring

$$\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\ \sum_{r,\alpha}\big\|\Phi^{(\varepsilon)}_{r,\alpha}\big\|_{L^2(\mu^{\otimes(r+1)})}^{2}<\infty .$$

The finite-degree data measured for the degree-14 five-point
relaxation is

| $\varepsilon$ | minimal Gram trace |
|---:|---:|
| $3\times10^{-1}$ | 4.543 |
| $1\times10^{-1}$ | 6.016 |
| $5\times10^{-2}$ | 7.269 |
| $3\times10^{-2}$ | no converged solution |

If that supremum is infinite the correct object is not a certificate
but an asymptotic family (the certificate-theorem asymptotic route of
[FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md)) — the certificate then exists only
as a limit, which is what the degree-14 evidence indicates.

## I.6 The search list

Find:

1. a countable family of leaf functions $\Phi_{r,\alpha}$ obeying the
   great-circle Fourier conditions (i) and (ii) on every circle
   compatible with their roots;
2. a pointwise nonnegative $P$, divisible by the three-point Gram
   determinant, whose quotient vanishes to order two on the three
   orthogonality lines;
3. a positive semidefinite tangent field $B$ annihilating
   $\operatorname{Hess}U_\mu$ on the support;
4. a nonnegative $\rho$ supported on $\{U_\mu=E\}$;
5. free multipliers $a_\mu$, $v_\mu$, $\Lambda_\mu$;

such that the expansion of all of them in the multigraph moment
algebra, modulo $\det\operatorname{Gram}_4=0$, reproduces the target
coefficient vector $\big(\tfrac{16}{3},\,-48,\,32\big)$ on
$\big(\text{constant},\,\mathrm{pair}_4,\,\mathrm{pair}_6\big)$.

# Part II. The (E1) equations solved: admissible leaves in closed form

Every claim in this part is verified in exact rational arithmetic by

```sh
python3 solve_e1.py            # checks (54 in Parts A–E; 69 with Part F)
python3 solve_e1.py --table    # + admissible-dimension tables
```

This carries out the selection principle of the certificate framework
([FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md)) — *solve the
complementary-slackness equations in closed form first, and only then
let an SDP choose the remaining coefficients* — instead of
extrapolating solver output.  The master equations are the circle-mode
conditions (E1) of Part I.

## II.0 Reductions making (E1) finite

Two reductions make (E1) finite and exactly computable:

* **Interior lemma.**  The truncated moment sets of admissible $\nu$
  have nonempty interior around Haar measure inside the slice
  $\{\hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0\}$, so "$=0$ for every admissible $\nu$"
  is equivalent to "$=0$ identically as a polynomial in the free
  moments $\tau_m=\int e^{im\theta}d\nu$" ($m$ even,
  $|m|\notin\{0,2,6\}$).
* **Configuration lemma.**  Up to rotation about $e$ and reflection,
  every root configuration is a tuple from $\{\pm e\}\cup C$ with
  symbolic equator angles; rotating a compatible circle about an
  equator root multiplies the spin-$k$ leaf integral by a phase
  $e^{ik\beta}$ and creates no new condition (checked exactly for
  $k\le3$: the "tilted circle" rows change nothing).

Everything below is the exact nullspace of the resulting linear
systems, over $\mathbb Q$, with no floating point.

## II.1 One-root leaves: a seven-dimensional space, at every degree

A spin-$k$ leaf about a root $x$ is $\Phi=f(t)\,w^k$ with $t=x\cdot y$
and $w=\sin\alpha\,e^{i\psi}$ (the solver's `flag_k` blocks pair these
through the Chebyshev kernel $R_k$).  Solving (E1):

| spin $k$ | admissible radial $f$ | dim |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 | $\operatorname{span}\{\,T_2+\tfrac13,\;T_6+\tfrac13\,\}$ | 2 |
| 1 | $\operatorname{span}\{\,t,\;U_5\,\}$ | 2 |
| 2 | $\operatorname{span}\{\,1,\;(4t^2-1)^2\,\}$ | 2 |
| 3 | $\operatorname{span}\{\,t^3\,\}$ | 1 |
| $\ge4$ | $\{0\}$ | 0 |

**The dimensions are independent of the degree cap** (stable from cap
8 through 24, and provably for all degrees: the conditions pin the
Chebyshev support).  Structural facts, all verified:

* $K=(T_2+\tfrac13)+(T_6+\tfrac13)$: the kernel itself is the sum of
  the two admissible spin-0 leaves.  Their rooted averages are
  $\int(T_2+\tfrac13)(x\cdot y)\,d\mu(y)=2x^{\mathsf T}(M-\tfrac13I)x$
  (the deviatoric quadratic) and the analogous hexapole average.
* The spin-1 space is exactly the $\theta$-derivative of the spin-0
  space ($\partial_\theta T_2=-2\sin\theta\,\cdot 2t$,
  $\partial_\theta T_6=-6\sin\theta\,U_5$): the admissible gradient
  flags are the gradients of the admissible potentials.
* Spin 3 survives on $t^3$ alone — $\sin^3\theta\cos^3\theta=
  \tfrac{1}{32}(3\sin2\theta-\sin6\theta)$, the $z_1z_2z_3$ structure
  of the ONB potential.  The surviving odd spins $\{1,3\}$ are
  precisely the regular orders $m\in\{1,3\}$ whose pole–equator faces
  do *not* annihilate the target (zero-energy structure,
  [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md)).
* Spins $\ge4$ die entirely; for spin 4 the one candidate
  $(1-t^2)^2f=\tfrac1{16}(8-9T_2+T_6)$, i.e. $f\propto 1+2t^2$, is
  killed by the pole-rooted condition $f(0)=0$ — the $\hat K_4<0$
  obstruction in leaf form.

So the entire one-root layer of any sharp certificate is at most
**seven-dimensional**, at every degree: Gram blocks of sizes
$2,2,2,1$.  Raising the SDP degree adds nothing to this layer — the
degree escalation observed numerically must flow through the two-root
layer and the KKT multipliers.

## II.2 Unrooted families

Evaluated on the whole family with symbolic $\nu$:

* $g_2=\iint P_2\,d\mu\,d\mu$ **vanishes identically** on the family
  ($\mu^*$ is isotropic): the $h_2$ face.  $g_4$ and $g_6$ do not:
  `harmonic_4`, `harmonic_6`, ... are dead in any sharp certificate.
* **Pair-power flags** (`empty_type_flag`): the only admissible
  combinations of $\{p_0,p_2,\dots,p_{12}\}$ are
  $$\operatorname{span}\{\,3p_2-1,\;\;E\,\}$$
  — the isotropy deficit and the target itself.  (Dim 2, exactly.)
* **Harmonic-weighted pair flags** (`harmonic_flag_l`): a weight
  combination survives iff its inner average
  $F_c(x)=\int\big(\sum_a c_a(x\cdot y)^a\big)d\mu^*(y)$ kills the
  order-$l$ pairing; the admissible spaces are exactly
  $$\operatorname{span}\{T_2+\tfrac13,\;T_6+\tfrac13\}\ (l\ge4),\qquad
  \operatorname{span}\{1,\;T_2+\tfrac13,\;T_6+\tfrac13\}\ (l=2),$$
  i.e. the (E1) spin-0 radials again — the leaves whose $\mu^*$-average
  vanishes on $\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$.  These blocks add no leaf
  freedom beyond §II.1.
* **Spin-2 deviatoric flags** (`spin2_flag`): 9 of 10 directions
  survive at degree cap 6 — the deviatoric pairing dies on the
  isotropic family, so this block passes facial reduction nearly
  whole.  Consistent with $h_2$ being the leak variable of the
  $\varepsilon$-pole.
* The **uniform global-gap multiplier** is dead by (E3): at the ONB,
  $\int(U-E)\,d\sigma=\tfrac{32}{105}>0$, so its multiplier must
  vanish in any certificate sharp at the ONB (`--check-onb` shows the
  0.3048).

## II.3 Two-root blocks and the coplanar chart

A **two-root block** is a square in which two points ("roots") are
sampled from $\mu$ and held as parameters, and the squared quantity is
a $\mu$-average over a third point (the "leaf"):

$$\mathcal Q[A]\;=\;\iint_{S^2\times S^2}
\Big(\int_{S^2} A(t_1,t_2,s)\,d\mu(y)\Big)^{2}
\,d\mu(x_1)\,d\mu(x_2)\;\ge\;0,
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
t_1&=x_1\cdot y,\\ t_2&=x_2\cdot y,\\ s&=x_1\cdot x_2,
\end{aligned}$$

for a polynomial $A(t_1,t_2,s)$.  Expanding the square produces a
linear combination of four-point moments of $\mu$ — the labels of the
SDP.  In `sos_search.py` these are the `two_root_*` sectors (basis
monomials $t_1^it_2^js^k$, Gram matrices over them); the
orientation-odd sectors use leaves $\det(x_1,x_2,y)\,B(t_1,t_2,s)$,
and the `_minor` sectors carry the extra root weight $1-s^2$.  The
one-root blocks are the special case where $A$ does not depend on
$x_2$.  A general two-root leaf is
$\Psi=A(t_1,t_2,s)+\det(x_1,x_2,y)\,B(t_1,t_2,s)$.

For a two-root block, (E1) reads

$$\int A(x_1\cdot y,\;x_2\cdot y,\;x_1\cdot x_2)\,d\mu^*(y)=0
\qquad\text{for all roots } x_1,x_2\in\operatorname{supp}\mu^* .$$

The conditions come from five configuration classes: both roots on the
equator at symbolic separation $\delta$ (b2), one root at a pole (a2
and its mirror), and both roots at poles ($s=\pm1$; dropped for the
`_minor` sectors, whose root weight $1-s^2$ vanishes there).

The decisive configuration puts both roots on the equator.  Place
$x_2$ at angle $\delta$ from $x_1$ along $C$ and the leaf $y$ at angle
$\theta$ from $x_1$; then

$$t_1=\cos\theta,\qquad t_2=\cos(\theta-\delta),\qquad s=\cos\delta ,$$

and the configuration lies on the coplanar variety $\{D=0\}$ with
$D=\det\operatorname{Gram}(x_1,x_2,y)=1+2t_1t_2s-t_1^2-t_2^2-s^2$.
Because $\nu$ is free except for $\hat\nu(2)=\hat\nu(6)=0$, the
condition forces the restriction of $A$ to this circle to be a
combination of the waves

$$\cos(2\theta-n\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad\cos(6\theta-n\delta),
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,$$

plus a mean term tied to the value of $A$ at the poles.  The integer
$n$ — how fast the wave precesses with the root separation $\delta$ —
is the direction along which "SDP degree" grows: a degree-$d$ block
can reach modulations $|n|\lesssim d$ only.  The generators below are
canonical polynomial representatives of these waves, corrected so that
the pole-tie also holds.

## II.4 The even sector, solved

1. **Profile structure.**  The restriction of an admissible $A$ to the
   circle has $\theta$-modes $\{0,\pm2,\pm6\}$ only, with free
   $\delta$-profiles on modes $2$ and $6$ and the mode-0 profile tied
   to the pole values.  The kernel of the profile map is exactly the
   family
   $$A\;=\;D\,Q\;-\;\tfrac13(1-s^2)\,Q(0,0,s),$$
   with $Q$ subject to the two pole-root slice conditions.  Verified
   as an exact dimension identity at degree 8:
   $\dim(\text{admissible})=52=26\ (\text{kernel family})+26\ (\text{profile rank})$.
2. **Canonical modulated generators** $\hat C_n$, $\hat S_n$
   (§§II.5–II.7): every integer $\delta$-modulation of the two
   surviving circle modes is realizable, with degree growing linearly
   in $|n|$; the arity-1 leaves are the $n=0$ members.
3. **Pure ideal part.**  $D\,Q$ is admissible iff $Q(0,0,s)\equiv0$
   and both slices $Q(0,t,0)$, $Q(t,0,0)$ lie in
   $\operatorname{span}\{2t^4-t^2\}$ (from
   $(T_2-T_6)/(1-t^2)=8t^2(2t^2-1)$).  In particular
   $D\cdot t_1t_2\cdot(\text{anything of matching parity})$ is
   admissible.
4. **The obstruction, localized.**  Products
   $(T_2+\tfrac13)(t_1)\,(T_2+\tfrac13)(t_2)$ of admissible arity-1
   leaves are *not* admissible: their circle restriction has mode
   $4=2+2$.  This is the Delsarte/two-point obstruction reappearing as
   a mode-selection rule.

## II.5 The mode-2 family $C_n$

**Definition.**

$$C_0=T_2(t_1)=2t_1^2-1,\qquad C_1=2t_1t_2-s,\qquad
\boxed{\;C_{n+1}=2s\,C_n-C_{n-1}\;}$$

(run backwards for $n<0$).  On the coplanar circle $C_n$ restricts to
$\cos(2\theta-n\delta)$: the seed $C_1$ comes from
$\cos(2\theta-\delta)=\cos\big(\theta+(\theta-\delta)\big)
=t_1t_2-\sin\theta\sin(\theta-\delta)$ together with
$\sin\theta\sin(\theta-\delta)=s-t_1t_2$, and the recurrence is
$2\cos\delta\cos(2\theta-n\delta)=\cos(2\theta-(n{+}1)\delta)
+\cos(2\theta-(n{-}1)\delta)$, kept polynomial because $\cos\delta=s$
is a variable.  Explicitly (check `explicit expansions of C_2, C_3,
C_4, C_-1`):

$$\begin{aligned}
C_{-1}&=4st_1^2-2t_1t_2-s,\\
C_2&=4st_1t_2-2s^2-2t_1^2+1,\\
C_3&=8s^2t_1t_2-4s^3-4st_1^2-2t_1t_2+3s,\\
C_4&=16s^3t_1t_2-8s^4-8s^2t_1^2-8st_1t_2+8s^2+2t_1^2-1 .
\end{aligned}$$

**Lift ambiguity.**  A polynomial representing a given function on the
coplanar variety is unique only modulo the coplanarity relation
$D=1+2t_1t_2s-t_1^2-t_2^2-s^2$, which vanishes there.  For example
$\cos(2\theta-2\delta)=T_2(t_2)$ on the circle, and indeed (check
`C_2 = T_2(t_2) + 2 D`)

$$C_2=T_2(t_2)+2D .$$

The recurrence merely fixes one convenient representative per wave;
the $D$-multiples are accounted separately (the "$(M,Q)$ kernel
family" of §II.4).

**Closed-form solution of the recurrence** (check `closed form C_n =
...`): in the Chebyshev basis of the $s$-recurrence,

$$C_n=C_0\,T_n(s)+2t_1(t_2-st_1)\,U_{n-1}(s),\qquad
S_n=S_0\,T_n(s)+(S_1-sS_0)\,U_{n-1}(s),$$

whence on the Gram body $|\hat C_n|\le\tfrac43+4n$ and
$|\hat S_n|\le\tfrac43+12n$: the theta atoms
$\Theta_q=\sum_nq^{n^2}\hat C_n$ converge for every $|q|<1$ (wrapper
lemma L7, [ENRICHMENTS.md](ENRICHMENTS.md)).

**One-step lifts in general.**  For every $m\ge2$, exactly on the
coplanar circle (check `one-step lifts ... for m = 2..7`):

$$\cos(m\theta-\delta)=U_{m-1}(t_1)\,t_2-U_{m-2}(t_1)\,s,
\qquad
\cos(m\theta+\delta)=U_m(t_1)\,s-U_{m-1}(t_1)\,t_2,$$

with $U_k$ the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
($U_k(\cos\theta)=\sin((k{+}1)\theta)/\sin\theta$).  For $m=2$ these
reproduce $C_1$ and $C_{-1}$.

## II.6 The pole-value lemma and the $\tfrac13T_n(s)$ correction

The raw $C_n$ is **not** yet (E1)-admissible.  When the leaf $y$ sits
at a pole, $t_1=t_2=0$, and (check `pole-value lemma`)

$$C_n(0,0,s)\;=\;-\,T_n(s)\qquad(\text{likewise } S_n(0,0,s)=-T_n(s)).$$

The full (E1) condition for the equator-pair configuration is

$$\tfrac13\,\big[\text{value at poles}\big]
+\tfrac23\,\big[\text{mode-0 of the circle restriction}\big]=0 ,$$

and the circle restriction $\cos(2\theta-n\delta)$ has zero mean.
Adding a function $g(s)$ of the root separation alone (mean
$g(\cos\delta)$, pole value $g(s)$) and solving
$\tfrac13\!\left(-T_n+g\right)+\tfrac23\,g=0$ gives $g=\tfrac13T_n$.
Hence the admissible generators:

$$\boxed{\;\hat C_n=C_n+\tfrac13\,T_n(s),\qquad
\hat S_n=S_n+\tfrac13\,T_n(s),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z\;}$$

(checks `modulated families ... admissible for every n` and
`C^_-1, C^_-2, S^_-1 are admissible`; the remaining configuration
classes — one root at a pole, both roots at poles — are verified to
hold as well).  Explicitly:

$$\begin{aligned}
\hat C_0&=2t_1^2-\tfrac23\;=\;(T_2+\tfrac13)(t_1),\\
\hat C_1&=2t_1t_2-\tfrac23\,s,\\
\hat C_2&=4st_1t_2-\tfrac43\,s^2-2t_1^2+\tfrac23,\\
\hat C_3&=8s^2t_1t_2-\tfrac83\,s^3-4st_1^2-2t_1t_2+2s,\\
\hat C_4&=16s^3t_1t_2-\tfrac{16}3\,s^4-8s^2t_1^2-8st_1t_2
+\tfrac{16}3\,s^2+2t_1^2-\tfrac23 .
\end{aligned}$$

$\hat C_0$ is precisely the admissible one-root leaf $T_2+\tfrac13$:
the seven-dimensional one-root layer of §II.1 is the $n=0$ slice of
these families.

## II.7 The mode-6 family $S_n$

Same recurrence, seeded at the surviving circle mode 6 (check
`S_1 = U_5(t1) t2 - U_4(t1) s and S_-1 = ...`):

$$S_0=T_6(t_1),\qquad
S_1=U_5(t_1)\,t_2-U_4(t_1)\,s,\qquad
S_{n+1}=2s\,S_n-S_{n-1},$$

with $U_5(t)=32t^5-32t^3+6t$, $U_4(t)=16t^4-12t^2+1$, and backwards
$S_{-1}=U_6(t_1)\,s-U_5(t_1)\,t_2$, $U_6(t)=64t^6-80t^4+24t^2-1$.
Corrected members:

$$\begin{aligned}
\hat S_0&=T_6(t_1)+\tfrac13,\\
\hat S_1&=U_5(t_1)\,t_2-U_4(t_1)\,s+\tfrac13\,s,\\

\end{aligned}$$

## II.8 A worked verification: $\hat C_1$

$\hat C_1=2t_1t_2-\tfrac23 s$ against every configuration class of
$\mu^*=\frac13\delta_{\pm e}+\frac23\nu$:

* **Both roots on the equator** (separation $\delta$).  On the circle:
  $2\cos\theta\cos(\theta-\delta)-\tfrac23\cos\delta
  =\cos(2\theta-\delta)+\tfrac13\cos\delta$; the wave integrates to
  zero against every admissible $\nu$ (it is a mode-2 wave and
  $\hat\nu(2)=0$), leaving $\tfrac13\cos\delta$.  At the poles
  $t_1=t_2=0$, so $\hat C_1=-\tfrac23\cos\delta$.  Total:
  $\tfrac13\big({-\tfrac23\cos\delta}\big)
  +\tfrac23\big(\tfrac13\cos\delta\big)=0$ for every $\delta$ and
  every admissible $\nu$.
* **One root at a pole** ($s=0$): $\hat C_1=2t_1t_2$; on the equator
  $t_1=0$, at the poles $t_2=0$ — every term vanishes pointwise.
* **Both roots at poles** ($x_2=\pm x_1$, $s=\pm1$, $t_2=\pm t_1$):
  $\hat C_1=\pm(2t_1^2-\tfrac23)$; equator value $\mp\tfrac23$, pole
  value $\pm\tfrac43$; total
  $\tfrac13(\pm\tfrac43)+\tfrac23(\mp\tfrac23)=0$.

## III.0 Verdicts up front

1. **Classification (pure squares).**  A two-root leaf
   $\Phi=A(t_1,t_2,s)+\det(x_1,x_2,y)\,B(t_1,t_2,s)$ has
   $E_\mu[\text{CE-square}]=0$ for *every* isotropic $\mu$ iff
   $$A=\alpha(s)\,(t_1^2-\tfrac13)+\beta(s)\,(t_2^2-\tfrac13)
      +\gamma(s)\,(t_1t_2-\tfrac s3),\qquad
     B=\lambda(s)\,t_1+\nu(s)\,t_2 ,$$
   i.e. iff $\Phi=y^{\mathsf T}C(x_1,x_2)\,y$ for a root-equivariant
   traceless symmetric $C$.  Equivalently
   $E_\mu[\Phi\mid x_1,x_2]=\langle C(x_1,x_2),\mathrm{Dev}(\mu)\rangle$
   with $\mathrm{Dev}=\int yy^{\mathsf T}d\mu-\tfrac13I$: every
   admissible pure-square two-root block is a root-modulated pairing
   against the deviatoric tensor, and its expectation lies in the
   ideal generated by the five deviatoric coordinates.
2. **The mode-2 theta tower survives; mode 6 dies.**  By
   $T_n=sU_{n-1}-U_{n-2}$,
   $$\hat C_n=-2\,U_{n-2}(s)\,(t_1^2-\tfrac13)+2\,U_{n-1}(s)\,(t_1t_2-\tfrac s3),$$
   so every modulated mode-2 generator $\hat C_n$ — hence every theta
   atom $\Theta_q^{(2)}$ — is leaf-quadratic and stays
   weighted-admissible.  The mode-6 tower $\hat S_n$ (leaf degree 6)
   is cut from the pure layer entirely.
3. **The $h_2$-localized layer is *not* unconstrained.**  Value-level
   slackness is vacuous for $h_2\times$blocks ($h_2=0$ on the zero
   set), but **second-order** slackness at the $E$-zero family forces
   the *base value* of every $h_2$-multiplied block to vanish there:
   the h2loc layer must satisfy the **unweighted (E1)** conditions
   (§III.5).  At full sharpness the same expansion kills every
   Dev-linear pure square and the scalar $h_2$-multiplier — the sharp
   weighted certificate is carried almost entirely by the localized
   module.
4. **The projection does not, and cannot, kill the pole.**  Facial
   projection removes dual generators, i.e. *weakens* the moment-side
   constraints: every recession ray survives projection.  Measured at
   degree 14 (MOSEK dual, all-measures cone): baseline
   $-1.9198\times10^{-4}$; pure-layer weighted-(E1) projection →
   **dual unbounded** (recession ray found, collision-type `graph_4`

   **$\ge95\%$ inside the admissible cone** at the smallest

   classification's operative use is therefore structural — it pins
   the sharp limit object and certifies which added objects

   not a finite-degree bound accelerator.

## III.1 The zero set of $W=h_2E$ and the affine-hull lemma

$W=h_2E$ with
$h_2=\frac{3p_2-1}2=\frac32\operatorname{tr}\mathrm{Dev}^2\ge0$.
Zero set: $\{W=0\}=\{h_2=0\}\cup\{E=0\}$, and $\{h_2=0\}$ = all
isotropic antipodal measures; the known $E$-zero family
(pole–equator) is itself isotropic, so $\{E=0\}\subset\{h_2=0\}$
among the known zeros.  So the *unconditional* zero set available to
complementary slackness is the full isotropic slice — codimension 5,
vastly larger than the unweighted zero family.

**Lemma (affine hull).**  For every truncation degree, the moment
vectors of isotropic antipodal probability measures fill a relatively
open neighborhood of Haar's moment point inside the affine slice
$$\{m_0=1,\ m_2=0,\ m_\ell\ \text{free for even }\ell\ge4\}$$
of harmonic moment coordinates $m_\ell=(\int Y_{\ell k}\,d\mu)_k$.
*Proof.*  Densities $\mu_q=(1+q)\sigma$ with $q$ an even polynomial,
$\langle q,1\rangle=\langle q,Y_{2k}\rangle=0$, $\|q\|_\infty<1$: such
$\mu_q$ is an isotropic antipodal probability measure and
$m_\ell(\mu_q)=\langle Y_{\ell\cdot},q\rangle$ realizes every small
value of the free coordinates. $\square$

**Consequence.**  A label functional (any $E_\mu[\cdot]$ of the flag
algebra, polynomial in $m$) vanishes on all isotropic measures iff its
polynomial vanishes identically after substituting $m_0=1$, $m_2=0$ —
iff it lies in the ideal generated by the five deviatoric coordinates
(and $m_0-1$).  This is the exact form of the statement
"$e(\text{square})$ annihilates the affine hull of
$\{y(\mu):M_2(\mu)=I/3\}$", and it is what `solve_e1.py` Part F
computes: the `IsotropicSlice` engine parametrizes the slice by
$\varepsilon$-symbols per even harmonic basis element of degree
$\ge4$, evaluates any multi-sample moment polynomial exactly on it,
and demands the zero polynomial.

## III.3 The arity-2 classification theorem

**Theorem.**  For a two-root flag square (any of the solver sectors
`two_root_*`, with or without the $(1-s^2)$ minor root weight), the
following are equivalent:

  (a) $E_\mu[\,(\text{conditional-expectation square})\,]=0$ for every
      isotropic antipodal probability measure $\mu$;
  (b) $E_\rho[\Phi(x_1,x_2;\cdot)]=0$ for every isotropic antipodal
      probability $\rho$ and **every** root pair
      $(x_1,x_2)\in S^2\times S^2$;
  (c) for every fixed root pair, $y\mapsto\Phi(x_1,x_2;y)$ is a pure
      degree-2 spherical harmonic;
  (d) $A=\alpha(s)(t_1^2-\tfrac13)+\beta(s)(t_2^2-\tfrac13)
      +\gamma(s)(t_1t_2-\tfrac s3)$ and $B=\lambda(s)t_1+\nu(s)t_2$
      with polynomial $\alpha,\dots,\nu$;
  (e) $\Phi=y^{\mathsf T}C(x_1,x_2)y$ on the spheres, with
      $$C=\alpha\,(x_1x_1^{\mathsf T}-\tfrac13I)
        +\beta\,(x_2x_2^{\mathsf T}-\tfrac13I)
        +\gamma\,(\mathrm{sym}(x_1x_2^{\mathsf T})-\tfrac s3 I)
        +\lambda\,\mathrm{sym}(x_1n^{\mathsf T})
        +\nu\,\mathrm{sym}(x_2n^{\mathsf T}),\quad n=x_1\times x_2 .$$

*Necessity* (a)$\Rightarrow$(b): squares are nonnegative, so (a)
forces the conditional expectation to vanish at $\mu$-a.e. root pair;
running $\mu=(1-t)\sigma+t\rho$ (isotropic for every isotropic $\rho$,
full support) decouples the roots from the measure: first $t=0$ gives
the Haar rows at all roots, then linearity in $t$ gives
$E_\rho[\Phi]=0$ for all roots and all isotropic $\rho$.
(b)$\Rightarrow$(c) is the affine-hull lemma applied in the leaf
variable.  (c)$\Rightarrow$(d): for fixed $s\in(-1,1)$ the pair
$(t_1,t_2)$ ranges over an open planar region, so the polynomial
identity in $(t_1,t_2)$ holds coefficient-wise, and in $s$ by density;
the $\det$-even/odd split matches the five quadratic harmonics
available in the frame $(x_1,x_2,n)$.  *Sufficiency*
(e)$\Rightarrow$(a): free polynomial identities (machine-checked
modulo the three sphere ideals, with $\operatorname{tr}C=0$ on the
root spheres) give
$E_\rho[\Phi]=\operatorname{tr}(C\,M_2(\rho))
=\langle C,\mathrm{Dev}(\rho)\rangle=0$ at isotropic $\rho$, pointwise
in the roots — so the square's expectation vanishes.  The minor
sectors carry the extra root factor $1-s^2\ge0$, which vanishes only
on the measure-zero set $s=\pm1$; the same equivalences hold verbatim.

**Machine checks** (Part F of `solve_e1.py`; the "squeeze"):
predicted $\subseteq$ nullspace of exact necessary rows (harmonic

## III.5 Second-order slackness: the localized layer's law, and a stronger collapse

Value-level slackness says nothing about $h_2\times$blocks ($h_2=0$ on
the whole zero set).  But let $\mu^*$ be in the $E$-zero family and
$\mu_t=\mu^*+td+O(t^2)$ an admissible curve of probability measures.
Then $h_2(\mu_t)=\tfrac12\ddot h_2[d]\,t^2+O(t^3)$ (as $h_2\ge0$
vanishes quadratically, $\ddot h_2[d]=3\|\mathrm{Dev}(d)\|_F^2\ge0$)
and $E(\mu_t)=t\dot E[d]+O(t^2)$, so
$$W(\mu_t)=O(t^3)\quad\text{along every curve through the } E\text{-zero family}.$$
For a sharp certificate $W=\sum_bV_b$ (+identities) with every
$V_b\ge0$ valid for all measures, $V_b(\mu_t)\ge0$ and
$\sum_bV_b(\mu_t)=O(t^3)$ force the $t^0,t^1,t^2$ coefficients of
each $V_b$ to vanish.  The $t^2$ coefficients are:

* $h_2$-localized block $V=h_2\cdot B_{\text{base}}$:
  $\tfrac12\ddot h_2[d]\,B_{\text{base}}(\mu^*)$.  Choosing $d$ with
  $\ddot h_2[d]>0$:
  $$\boxed{\;B_{\text{base}}(\mu^*)=0\ \text{for every }\mu^*\text{ in
  the }E\text{-zero family}\;}$$
  — the **unweighted (E1) conditions** on the *base* of every
  localized block.  The h2loc two-root layer must live in the
  unweighted admissible bases (19/20/28/28 + minors 13/13/19/19), the
  h2loc spin-2 layer in its 9/10 space, the h2loc pair layer in
  $\{3p_2-1,E\}$.
* pure square $V=E[(\mathrm{CE})^2]$: $\int(\partial_d\mathrm{CE})^2$,
  so $\partial_d\mathrm{CE}(R)=0$ for $\mu^*$-a.e. roots and every
  direction.  With $\mathrm{CE}=\langle C(R),\mathrm{Dev}(\mu)\rangle$
  and $\mathrm{Dev}(d)$ spanning all traceless directions, this kills
  $C$ on $(\operatorname{supp}\mu^*)^2$ for every family member, hence
  $C\equiv0$: **at full sharpness every Dev-linear pure square dies**
  (two-root, one-root, and the Dev-linear spin-2 combinations).  The
  survivors are the Dev-quadratic squares — $(3p_2-1)$-type — whose
  CE vanishes to second order.
* scalar $h_2$ multiplier $\lambda\,g_2$:
  $\lambda\cdot\tfrac13\ddot h_2[d]$, so $\lambda=0$: even $h_2$
  itself cannot appear linearly; the $t^3$ budget
  $\tfrac12\ddot h_2[d]\,\dot E[d]$ is carried by the localized layer
  through its base *derivatives*.

So the sharp weighted certificate is, up to Dev-quadratic scalars,
**entirely the $h_2$-localized module over the unweighted-(E1)
bases**.  The value-level weighted-(E1) classification (§III.3)
remains the correct necessary condition for whatever pure squares are
kept at finite degree, and the second-order law is implemented as an
independent projection of the localized layer (§III.6).  The full
second-order system away from the $E$-zero stratum (at isotropic
$\mu^*$ with $E(\mu^*)>0$ there is positive budget
$\tfrac12\ddot h_2E(\mu^*)$) is the (E2) analogue left to the
multiplier-layer program; it constrains the localized layer's base
*derivatives* and should determine the profile coefficients
$\gamma_n$ of the escaping tower.

# Part IV. Collective transport: the simultaneous-motion second variation

**Validity status: KKT-only.**  The block below is a necessary
condition for every local minimizer under smooth transport variations;
it is *not* valid for all measures, so it composes with the reduction
lemma only under the KKT-run caveat of §III.2.

The rooted Hessian constraints move one support point against a fixed
background measure.  A true minimizer is also stable when **all
support points move simultaneously** according to a tangent vector
field.  The resulting second variation contains off-diagonal terms
coupling the motions at two sampled points.

## IV.1 Transport variation

Let

$$E(\mu)=\iint K(x\cdot y)\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y),
\qquad
K(t)=32t^6-48t^4+20t^2-\frac43 .$$

Fix a tangent field $V(x)\perp x$.  Move each point along the
spherical geodesic with initial velocity $V(x)$:

$$x_t=\cos(t\|V(x)\|)x+
\frac{\sin(t\|V(x)\|)}{\|V(x)\|}V(x),
\qquad
\mu_t=(x\mapsto x_t)_*\mu .$$

At a local minimizer, $E'(0)=0$ and $E''(0)\ge0$.  For $s=x\cdot y$,
$v=V(x)$, and $w=V(y)$,

$$\frac12E''(0)=\iint\Big[
K''(s)(v\cdot y)^2-sK'(s)\|v\|^2
+K''(s)(v\cdot y)(x\cdot w)
+K'(s)v\cdot w
\Big]d\mu(x)d\mu(y).$$

The first two terms are the averaged pointwise support Hessian
already used by the hierarchy.  The last two are the new **collective
cross term**.  They measure whether two support points can lower the
energy by moving in a correlated way.

## IV.2 Polynomial vector fields

The initial implementation uses the projectively well-defined fields

$$V_r(x)=\mathbb E_Z[(x\cdot Z)^r P_{x^\perp}Z],
\qquad r=1,3,5,\ldots,
\qquad P_{x^\perp}Z=Z-(x\cdot Z)x .$$

Odd $r$ is forced by antipodal symmetry: the lifted field satisfies
$V_r(-x)=-V_r(x)$, as required for a tangent vector field on
$\mathbb{RP}^2$.

For $V=\sum_r c_rV_r$, the second variation is a quadratic form in the
coefficients $c_r$; therefore its moment matrix must be positive
semidefinite.

## IV.3 Four-point Gram formula

Use independent samples $X,Y,Z,W$ and the edge order

$$XY=a,\quad XZ=c,\quad XW=d,\quad
YZ=b,\quad YW=e,\quad ZW=f .$$

For the local term, set $v=P_{X^\perp}Z$, $u=P_{X^\perp}W$.  Then

$$L=
K''(a)(b-ac)(e-ad)
-aK'(a)(f-cd).$$

For the collective cross term, set $v=P_{X^\perp}Z$,
$w=P_{Y^\perp}W$.  The relevant contractions are

$$v\cdot Y=b-ac,
\qquad
X\cdot w=d-ae,
\qquad
v\cdot w=f-eb-cd+ace .$$

Hence

$$C=
K''(a)(b-ac)(d-ae)
+K'(a)(f-eb-cd+ace).$$

For basis powers $r,s$, the matrix entry is the expectation of

$$(X\cdot Z)^r(X\cdot W)^s L
+
(X\cdot Z)^r(Y\cdot W)^s C .$$

This is a polynomial in the six Gram entries, so it passes through the
same antipodal, isotropic-contraction, exchangeability, and rank-three
reduction machinery as the existing flag blocks.

## IV.4 Degree bookkeeping

Both base kernels have total Gram degree at most 8.  Therefore, with
an SDP degree cap $D$, a square matrix indexed by powers $r,s$ is
admissible when

$$r+s+8\le D .$$

The default symmetric choice is

$$r,s\in\{1,3,5,\ldots\},
\qquad
r,s\le\left\lfloor\frac{D-8}{2}\right\rfloor .$$

Thus the first nontrivial block appears at degree 10, and degree 14
gives the basis $[1,3]$.

## IV.5 Code and tests

`collective_transport.py` provides

```python
collective_transport_polynomials()

collective_transport_degrees(total_degree)
```

The expectation-matrix output has the same type as the existing
`four_point_hessian_expectation_matrix` output and is appended to the
solver's PSD `blocks` list:

```python
from collective_transport import (
    collective_transport_degrees,
    collective_transport_expectation_matrix,
)

transport_degrees = collective_transport_degrees(args.degree)
if args.collective_transport and transport_degrees:
    label_matrices = collective_transport_expectation_matrix(transport_degrees)
    variable = cp.Variable(
        (len(transport_degrees), len(transport_degrees)),
        symmetric=True,
        name="collective_transport_hessian",
    )
    blocks.append(("collective_transport_hessian", variable, label_matrices))
    constraints.append(variable >> 0)
```

The block is a necessary condition for every local minimizer under
smooth transport variations.  It does not require isotropy
analytically, although the moment reducer uses isotropy when
canonicalizing its entries.

`test_collective_transport.py` checks:

A natural extension is to replace the one-leaf fields $V_r$ by general
